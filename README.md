Hybrid Physics–AI Modelling for Vessel Fuel Consumption and Performance Degradation Detection
A focused methodological demonstration of hybrid physics-informed residual learning applied to vessel shaft-power prediction and hull-condition (biofouling-proxy) monitoring.
Author: Mohsen Zeynivand 
Status: Self-initiated methodological demonstration, May 2026.
---
Scope and honest framing
This repository accompanies a short technical report (`report/technical_report.docx`). It is a self-initiated methodological exercise, produced to deepen my engagement with hybrid physics-AI methods for maritime energy systems. It is not a substitute for work on real operational data and does not claim quantitative results that generalise beyond the synthetic case considered.
The synthetic dataset is built from documented naval-architecture relationships (admiralty-coefficient speed-power formulation, simplified weather-correction terms in the spirit of ISO 19030, and a logistic biofouling-progression model with a hull-cleaning reset). The data generator itself is the dataset's documentation — see `src/data_generation.py`. The intended next step is application of the same methodology to real operational data, which would be the focus of a research engagement on this topic.
What this repository demonstrates
A hybrid model architecture combining a physics-based shaft-power baseline with a gradient-boosting residual learner.
A comparison of three modelling strategies (physics-only, pure data-driven, hybrid) on the same synthetic operating record. On this case, the hybrid model achieves the lowest RMSE and MAPE while preserving component-wise interpretability.
A condition-monitoring signal derived from the hybrid model: a smoothed, weather-normalised excess-power indicator, with a one-sided CUSUM alarm that detects sustained drift consistent with progressive hull fouling and resets after a cleaning event.
Key results
On a 720-day synthetic operating record (handysize bulk carrier, broad weather and loading variability, fouling progression with a mid-record hull-cleaning event):
Model	RMSE (kW)	MAE (kW)	R²	MAPE (%)
Physics-only	433.7	337.0	0.890	6.6
Data-driven	937.9	737.2	0.484	16.3
Hybrid	397.8	269.8	0.907	5.2
The data-driven model is trained on the same clean-hull baseline window as the other models; its poor full-record performance illustrates the extrapolation weakness of a black-box learner when deployment conditions drift beyond the training distribution. The hybrid model inherits the physics model's extrapolation behaviour and uses the residual learner to capture short-scale corrections.
Degradation detection: the CUSUM-based alarm raises its first sustained signal around day 161 (with a fouling progression that begins at day 0), clears within weeks of the day-540 hull cleaning event, and re-triggers as fouling resumes.

Methodological notes
Why a residual-learning hybrid. Residual learning (`P_hybrid = P_physics + ML_residual`) is the most transparent of the common hybrid patterns: the physics model carries domain knowledge and extrapolates, while the ML component captures the unexplained part of the signal. Any prediction can be decomposed into "what the physics expected" and "what the ML corrected", which matters for maintenance acceptance and for debugging in deployment.
Why physics calibration on a baseline window matters. Fitting the physics coefficients (admiralty coefficient, wave/wind correction gains) on a clean-hull window grounds the model in a vessel-specific reference state. Subsequent residuals are interpretable as deviations from that reference.
Why CUSUM on the residual is a reasonable detector. Biofouling progression is monotonic between cleaning events. A one-sided CUSUM is the canonical detector for sustained positive drift in a centred, smoothed signal — simple, well-understood, with tunable false-alarm characteristics.
Limitations
The dataset is synthetic. The relationships in the data are exactly the ones the physics model uses, plus noise and a fouling drift. This means the demonstration shows the methodology clearly but does not constitute a benchmark of model performance on real operational data.
The physics baseline uses a simple admiralty-formula form. Real applications would benefit from a fuller propulsion-train model (engine map, propeller open-water characteristics, hull resistance decomposition).
The weather model is highly simplified. In real operations, wave direction relative to ship heading varies continuously and added-resistance estimates depend on hull form (Holtrop & Mennen, STAWAVE, or vessel-specific tables).
The degradation detector here uses a single CUSUM. Production systems would combine multiple indicators (speed–power curve drift, propeller pitch–power signature, hull and propeller condition separately per ISO 19030) and incorporate uncertainty estimates.
